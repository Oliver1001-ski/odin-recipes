CARBONARA RECIPE

Carbonara is a classic Roman pasta dish celebrated for its rich, creamy texture and deeply savory flavor profile. The traditional recipe relies on just a few high-quality ingredients: pasta, guanciale (cured pork cheek), egg yolks, Pecorino Romano cheese, and plenty of freshly cracked black pepper. Despite its luscious consistency, an authentic carbonara contains absolutely no cream; the velvety sauce is created entirely by emulsifying the raw eggs and finely grated cheese with starchy, hot pasta cooking water.

The magic of the dish lies in its precise execution and contrast of textures. Crispy, rendered pieces of guanciale provide a salty, fatty crunch that cuts through the velvety coating of the noodles, typically spaghetti or rigatoni. As the hot pasta is tossed away from direct heat with the egg and cheese mixture, the residual warmth gently cooks the egg into a smooth, glossy glaze without scrambling it. The result is a comforting, soul-satisfying balance of pork fat, sharp sheep's milk cheese, and a fiery kick of black pepper.

  1. Boil the pasta: Cook pasta in boiling salted water until it is slightly under al dente.
  2. Crisp the pork: Fry the guanciale in a large pan over medium heat until crispy and fat renders. Remove the pan from heat.
  3. Whisk the sauce base: Mix the egg yolks, whole egg, grated Pecorino, and black pepper in a bowl until a thick paste forms.
  4. Combine pasta and pork: Drain the pasta, reserving 1 cup of cooking water. Toss the hot pasta directly into the pan with the guanciale and its rendered fat.
  5. Emulsify the sauce: Let the pan cool slightly. Pour in the egg mix and a splash of cooking water. Toss vigorously away from direct heat until a creamy, glossy sauce coats the pasta.
